## Independent mathematical recomputation

### Period-two example

From the displayed
\[
M_p=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad e_1=(1,0)^\mathsf T,\quad e_2=(0,1)^\mathsf T,
\]
I independently obtain
\[
M_pe_1=e_2,\qquad M_pe_2=e_1,\qquad M_p^2e_1=e_1.
\]
Thus \(e_1,e_2,e_1,\ldots\) alternates and does not have a pointwise limit.  For \(r=(x,1-x)^\mathsf T\), stationarity \(M_pr=r\) gives \(1-x=x\), so the unique stationary probability is
\[
r_\star=(1/2,1/2)^\mathsf T.
\]
This verifies both the uniqueness claim and the distinct non-convergence claim; it does not confuse a stationary distribution with convergence from \(e_1\).

### Dangling-column example and repair order

For
\[
M_d=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
the column sums are \((1,0)\).  Hence its second column is zero,
\[
M_de_2=(0,0)^\mathsf T,\qquad
\boldsymbol1^\mathsf TM_de_2=0,
\]
so a state \(e_2\) of mass one is sent to mass zero.  This is an invalid stochastic transition, not merely a harmless numerical effect.

Here \(a=e_2\).  With any probability vector \(v=(v_1,v_2)^\mathsf T\),
\[
S=M_d+ve_2^\mathsf T
=\begin{pmatrix}0&v_1\\1&v_2\end{pmatrix}
\]
has column sums \((1,v_1+v_2)=(1,1)\).  Only after that repair,
\[
G=dS+(1-d)v\boldsymbol1^\mathsf T,\quad 0\le d<1,
\]
has \(\boldsymbol1^\mathsf TG=\boldsymbol1^\mathsf T\).  The diagram and surrounding prose use this required “repair \(S\), then damp \(G\)” order.
